## Pitch

Mark is 13, a rising Twitch streamer who's dealing with puberty and a popularity he never asked for. His sister Hannah, 15, used to be the family's golden child, and now she watches their parents rave about him. So she starts humiliating him with embarrassing clips and comments, and her mod powers make it spread faster. How far will she go to get her family's attention back, and what happens when breaking her brother is the only way she feels seen?


## What is the experience about

- **What is the dilemma of the main characters?**
  - **Hannah** wants her brother's pain to stop, and also wants it to continue, because it's the only thing that gets her looked at. She is the protector who becomes the saboteur while believing she is the victim.
  - **Mark** is being betrayed by the person meant to protect him, and he can't name it because she's family. The more people watch him, the less he recognises himself.
 
- **How does this dilemma relate to cyberbullying?**  Hannah's clips, comments and mod powers are the bullying tools, and the chat amplifies them. Mark is "marked": tagged, clipped, singled out, turned from "Mark" into "a mark".
- **How does the "cyber" component affect this dilemma?**
  - Every clip Hannah posts pushes Mark's view count up, so her cruelty feeds the success she resents.
  - Mark can't turn the audience off, even when he's shaking.
  - Hannah can't undo what the chat turns into once it has been clipped and shared.
 
## What is understood / What knowledge is learned

- **What tools adolescents can learn from your project**
  - Seeing that bullying can come from someone close, and that it's okay to name it.
  - Telling apart a bully's pain from their choices: understanding why someone hurts others doesn't excuse it.
  - Asking for help when sensory overload and public attention become too much.
- **How does the story internally lead to that understanding**
  - This is where empathy turns into understanding.
  - The ending puts the consequences of the chat's behaviour in front of the player.

## Agency

- Mark's agency: he can't switch off the audience, but he chooses to help Hannah.
- Hannah's agency is the final choice: Help mark, or leave mark.
