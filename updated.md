## Pitch

Hannah is 15, and at home she's disappearing. She used to be the golden child. Now every dinner is about her brother Mark, 13, a rising Twitch streamer. Mark is shooting up, his voice is breaking, and the house is full of his noise and his viewers. Hannah's puberty hasn't come yet. She still looks like a kid and gets treated like one. As Mark's moderator, she has everything she needs to make him slip: an embarrassing clip, a cruel comment, a mistake pinned for the whole chat to see. The game asks you to understand her, and then shows you what you've been understanding.

## What is the experience about

- **What is the dilemma of the main characters?**
  - **Hannah** wants her parents to see her again. She's jealous of Mark for taking their attention and for growing up while her own body hasn't, and that jealousy hardens into resentment. Hurting him is the one thing that makes her feel bigger than him.
  - **Mark** is being bullied by the person running his chat, and he can't name it because she's family. He doesn't rescue Hannah or fix her. He's just trying to get through each stream.

- **Theme: betraying the player's emotions.** The player is drawn to Hannah first: overlooked at home, waiting for her body to change, invisible next to her brother. The game earns that sympathy, then turns it around. The clips, the pile-ons and what the chat does to Mark all come from her. The player realises the person they felt for is the bully, and that understanding someone's pain doesn't mean taking their side.

- **How does this dilemma relate to cyberbullying?** Hannah's clips, comments and mod powers are the bullying tools, and the chat amplifies them. Mark is "marked": tagged, clipped, singled out, turned from "Mark" into "a mark".

- **How does the "cyber" component affect this dilemma?**
  - Every clip Hannah posts pushes Mark's view count up, so her cruelty feeds the success she resents.
  - Mark can't turn the audience off, even when he's shaking.
  - Once something is clipped and shared, Hannah can't control what the chat turns it into.

## What is understood / What knowledge is learned

- **What tools adolescents can learn from your project**
  - Bullying can come from someone close, and it's okay to name it.
  - Jealousy and loneliness can explain why someone hurts others, but they don't excuse it.
  - Feeling sorry for someone shouldn't stop you from seeing what they're doing.
  - Asking for help when public attention becomes too much.

- **How does the story internally lead to that understanding**
  - The player's sympathy for Hannah is built up, then betrayed. That moment turns empathy into understanding.
  - The ending (TBD) puts the consequences in front of the player.
